Why this question

What would a 1% improvement in OEE be worth to your business? Most people have never worked it out. For most manufacturers it's a very significant number.

You only need two figures from your annual report to start. Then add what you know to make the estimate sharper.

The defaults describe an example company so you can see how it works. They're not a forecast.

You don't need to know your OEE

Here, a 1% improvement means 1% more good output from the same equipment and hours. That's deliberately cautious. At 50% OEE, one extra point of OEE is actually 2% more output.

If you do have an OEE figure and it's captured by hand, treat it with care. Manual OEE usually misses short stops and slow running, so it tends to look better than it really is.

Research on smart manufacturing programmes reports gains well beyond a few percent. Deloitte's 2025 survey found 10% to 20% improvements in production output among large manufacturers. I pull the studies together in What the research says about smart factory ROI.

How it's calculated

An improvement of X% in OEE gives X% more good output from the same equipment and hours.

Selling more: revenue × share you make × X% × share you could sell × margin. It uses contribution margin if you enter it, and gross margin if you don't.

Costs you no longer incur: the output you can't sell is made in less time instead. That saves conversion costs × X ÷ (100 + X), on the share you can't sell. It only shows when you enter conversion costs.

The value is the sum of the two. It counts nothing for inventory, scrap or quality escapes avoided, so the real number is usually bigger.
