Why this question

What would a 1% improvement in OEE be worth to your business? Most people have never worked it out. For most manufacturers it's a very significant number.

You only need two figures from the annual report to start. Then pick your industry and add what you know to make the estimate sharper.

The defaults describe an example manufacturer with £1bn of revenue, so you can see how it works. They're not a forecast.

You don't need to know your OEE

Here, a 1% improvement means 1% more good output from the same equipment and hours. That's deliberately cautious. At 50% OEE, one extra point of OEE is actually 2% more output.

If you do have an OEE figure and it's captured by hand, treat it with care. Manual OEE usually misses short stops and slow running, so it tends to look better than it really is.

The improvement needs to land on the equipment that limits your output. Gains on a machine that's waiting for the one before it don't add output.

How it's calculated, and where the defaults come from

An improvement of X% in OEE gives X% more good output from the same equipment and hours, as long as it's on the equipment that limits output.

Selling more: revenue × share you make × X% × share you could sell × margin. It uses contribution margin if you enter it, and gross margin if you don't.

Costs you no longer incur: the output you can't sell is made in less time instead, which saves conversion costs × X ÷ (100 + X) on that share. If you don't enter conversion costs, they're estimated as cost of sales minus materials, on the products you make, times the share that falls with fewer hours.

The value is the sum of the two. It counts nothing for inventory, scrap or quality escapes avoided, which usually make the real number bigger.

Industry materials shares are cost of materials divided by value of shipments, from the US Census Bureau Annual Survey of Manufactures (2019 by industry; 2021 for all manufacturing). Census cost of materials includes purchased fuel and electricity. Shares in the UK and Europe will differ, so use your own accounts where you can.
