Why this question

In every business case conversation I ask the same thing: what would a 1% improvement in OEE be worth to your business? Most people have never worked it out. For most manufacturers it's a very significant number.

Use your own figures. The defaults describe an example mid-size site so you can see how it works. They're not a forecast.

Two ways a point of OEE pays

If you can sell more: the same lines make more good product in the same hours. That's extra contribution margin.

If you can't: you make the same output in fewer hours. That's overtime or weekend shifts you no longer need.

Research on smart manufacturing programmes reports output gains well beyond a few points. Deloitte's 2025 survey found 10% to 20% improvements in production output among large manufacturers. I pull the studies together in What the research says about smart factory ROI.

How it's calculated

Output is proportional to OEE, so one extra point raises good output by 1 ÷ current OEE. At 55% that's about 1.8% more output from the same hours.

Selling more: revenue × (1 ÷ OEE) × share you can sell × contribution margin.

Fewer hours: the share you can't sell is made in fewer hours instead. Hours freed = planned hours × (1 − OEE ÷ (OEE + 1 point)), multiplied by the unsold share and your running cost per hour.

The value is the sum of the two. It counts nothing for inventory or quality escapes avoided, so the real number is usually bigger.
