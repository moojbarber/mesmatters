## What is Kaizen in manufacturing?

Kaizen is a Japanese term that translates to "change for the better" or "continuous improvement." In the context of manufacturing, it refers to activities that continually improve all functions and involve all employees from the CEO to the assembly line workers. This philosophy is a core pillar of the Toyota Production System, which has influenced manufacturing practices globally.

Many factories nowadays have a continuous improvement (CI) team responsible for evaluating an improving manufacturing processes, and guess what....using an MES helps! We will dive into exactly how MES helps later on.

#### Origins of Kaizen

The concept of Kaizen was born in Japan following World War II. The term was coined by Toyota as a fundamental part of their production system. The Toyota Production System, often referred to as "Lean Manufacturing," aimed to eliminate waste and inefficiency in production processes. Kaizen was the method by which continuous, incremental improvements could be made.

While Kaizen originated in Japan, its principles have been adopted by businesses worldwide. It is now a common practice in industries ranging from healthcare to logistics, and from software development to education.

#### Principles of Kaizen

Kaizen is based on five fundamental principles:

1. Teamwork
2. Personal discipline
3. Improved morale
4. Quality circles
5. Suggestions for improvement.

These principles are designed to create a productive work environment where all employees are actively engaged in improving the company, or manufacturing process.

Teamwork is crucial as Kaizen believes that the collective efforts of all employees (not just those in managerial or supervisory roles) can lead to significant improvements. Personal discipline, on the other hand, is necessary for maintaining a commitment to these continuous improvements.

Improved morale and quality circles are related. By involving all employees in improvement activities, companies can boost morale and create a sense of ownership. Quality circles are small groups of employees who meet regularly to discuss and solve work-related problems.

The last principle, suggestions for improvement, encourages all employees to contribute ideas for bettering the company's processes, products, or services. These suggestions are valued, regardless of how small they may seem.

Everyone has a voice, which makes Kaizen incredibly powerful when implemented correctly.

#### Implementing Kaizen in Manufacturing

##### Step 1: Identify an Opportunity

The first step in implementing Kaizen is to identify an opportunity for improvement. This could be a process that is inefficient, a product that could be improved, or a service that could be more effectively delivered. The key is to focus on areas where there is clear room for improvement.

MES data can help define the opportunity, shining a light on the issues through increased visibility, and allowing data-driven decision making.

##### Step 2: Analyse the Process

Once an opportunity has been identified, the next step is to analyse the process. This involves mapping out the current process, measuring its performance, and identifying any waste or inefficiencies. The goal is to understand the process in detail so that you can identify the best ways to improve it.

Use MES to analyse the process and capture the relevant data.

##### Step 3: Develop a Plan

After analysing the process, the next step is to develop a plan for improvement. This plan should outline the changes that need to be made, the steps required to implement these changes, and the metrics that will be used to measure success.

Metrics will likely be captured, measured, and compared in MES.

##### Step 4: Implement the Plan

The fourth step is to implement the plan. This involves making the changes that have been identified, monitoring the process to ensure that the changes are having the desired effect, and making any necessary adjustments. The key to this step is to make changes gradually and continuously, rather than trying to make large changes all at once.

MES may support some of the changes, or manage some of the actions to be taken.

##### Step 5: Review and Start Again

The final step in the Kaizen process is to review the results and start again. This involves analysing the impact of the changes, learning from the process, and identifying new opportunities for improvement. The goal is to create a cycle of continuous improvement.

Some results will likely be reviewed using data from the MES.

#### Benefits of Kaizen in Manufacturing

Kaizen can lead to significant improvements in efficiency and productivity. By continuously identifying and eliminating waste, companies can reduce costs and increase output.

Another major benefit of Kaizen is that it can improve product quality. By focusing on continuous improvement, companies can identify and correct defects or inefficiencies that may be affecting the quality of their products.

Kaizen combined with MES gives the visibility of these improvements, by being able to accurately measure the results of changes.

Kaizen can have a positive impact on employee morale. By involving all employees in the improvement process, companies can create a more engaged and motivated workforce.

#### Challenges of Implementing Kaizen

While the benefits of Kaizen are clear, implementing it is not without its challenges. One of the main challenges is changing the mindset of employees. Kaizen requires a culture of continuous improvement, which can be difficult to establish.

Another challenge is maintaining momentum. It can be easy for companies to become complacent once initial improvements have been made. However, the key to Kaizen is continuous improvement, which means that companies must constantly be looking for new ways to improve.

Implementing Kaizen requires a commitment of time and resources. While the long-term benefits can be significant, it can take time to see results. Companies must be willing to invest in the process and be patient as improvements are made.

## The digital morning meeting

Here's a practical example of Kaizen with MES/MOM.

In most factories the morning meeting takes hours to prepare. Data from ERP, spreadsheets, separate reports and handwritten notes. And the first thirty minutes are spent arguing about whose numbers are right.

The digital version: one screen, one source of truth, real-time data. OEE by line, top downtime reasons, quality issues, schedule adherence, maintenance status, inventory, health and safety. No preparation. No arguments. The meeting starts with facts.

Actions are logged and tracked digitally, linked to the data that prompted them. Next week you can see whether they worked. That's PDCA (plan, do, check, act) running every single day.

## Conclusion

Kaizen is a powerful philosophy that can drive significant improvements in manufacturing. While it requires a commitment to continuous improvement and can be challenging to implement, the potential benefits in terms of increased efficiency, improved product quality, and higher employee morale make it a worthwhile goal for many manufacturing companies. MES supports many Kaizen initiatives, making it easier to drive continuous improvement activities.
