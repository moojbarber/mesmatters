Low-code platforms for manufacturing have grown fast over the last few years. Some vendors position them directly against traditional MES. Some MES vendors dismiss them as toys.

Neither view is fair. In my experience they're built for different kinds of business, and tackle problems at opposite ends of the market.

## What a low-code platform is

A low-code platform gives your own people the tools to build shopfloor applications without writing much code. Drag-and-drop builders for screens and logic, plus connectors to machines and other systems. You decide what to build, and your team builds it.

Done well, it's fast. In my experience, configuring something in a low-code environment is far quicker than coding it from scratch. A digital checklist or a downtime capture screen can be live in weeks.

## What an MES/MOM platform is

An MES/MOM platform arrives with the operation already modelled: orders, routings, materials, genealogy, quality, inventory and maintenance, on one data model. You configure it to your processes rather than building each application.

Most modern platforms also include low-code tools for screens, reports, dashboards and workflows. So the real difference is how much is already built for you before you start, rather than whether you can use low-code.

## Side by side

| | Low-code platform | MES/MOM platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Starting point** | A toolkit to build with | A product to configure |
| **Speed to first app** | Very fast | Moderate |
| **Breadth** | Whatever you build | Production, quality, inventory and maintenance, ready to switch on |
| **Data model** | You design it | Built in and shared |
| **Best practice and standard KPIs** | Yours to build in | Largely built in |
| **Skills needed** | A capable internal team, for the long term | Configuration skills, and a strong project team |
| **Typical fit** | Smaller companies, tech-savvy teams, narrow scope | Larger or multi-site operations wanting a standard way of working |

## Where low-code shines

- **Speed and ownership.** Engineers can solve their own problems without waiting for a vendor or an IT queue.
- **Unusual or niche needs.** Something no product handles well is often quicker to build than to bend a product around.
- **Proving value.** A quick app can build trust with the shopfloor and show what digital can do.
- **Lower cost to start.** Licences are often modest, and the people may already be on the payroll.

## What to watch

- **It relies heavily on internal skills.** Every app needs designing, testing, documenting and supporting for its whole life.
- **Apps can multiply.** It's easy to end up with a collection of apps solving narrow problems, rather than a joined-up operation.
- **The data model is yours.** Context only exists if someone designs it and keeps it consistent across every app.
- **Scale changes the picture.** Rolling out a standard way of working across many sites is a very different job from building one plant's apps.

## Using both

Plenty of manufacturers combine them. MES/MOM runs the core operation on one data model, and low-code fills the gaps at the edges, such as a site-specific form or an improvement idea someone wants to try. If your MES/MOM includes its own low-code tools, those edge apps can share the same data rather than sitting beside it.

Whichever way you go, start small, but start small on the right path, with the destination in mind.
