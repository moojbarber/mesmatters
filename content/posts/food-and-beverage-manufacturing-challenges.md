Food and beverage manufacturing combines perishable materials with some of the strictest safety expectations in industry. Alongside developing new products, manufacturers have to manage a set of operational challenges that shape their daily work and long-term competitiveness.

This article looks at the main challenges, and where shopfloor systems such as MES/MOM help.

## Food safety and quality

Food safety is the top priority. A lapse can put consumers at risk, and a single recall brings the cost of withdrawn product and lasting damage to a brand.

Control has to run from the arrival of raw ingredients to the finished pack. That includes allergen management and accurate labelling, all within changing regulatory requirements. More in [allergen management](/glossary/allergen-management).

## Waste and give-away

Ingredient and energy prices move with global markets, and demand shifts with seasons and trends. Waste in food and beverage takes many forms:

- Spoilage of perishable materials
- Energy lost in heating and cooling
- Over-production that expires before it's sold
- Batches reworked because they missed specification on taste or appearance

**Give-away**, product packed above the declared weight to stay safe, is a particular issue. Small amounts per pack add up across millions of units. More in [give-away](/glossary/give-away).

## Yield and recipe control

Yield from raw materials is often the largest single profit driver, so small improvements can have a large return. Manual processes add variability: inconsistent weighing, and recipes adjusted from experience rather than data.

## Workforce and skills

Attracting and keeping skilled people is hard across manufacturing, and food and beverage is no exception. Modern equipment and precise processes need people who follow the right procedure every time. Paper-based training and instructions slow down onboarding and make consistency harder, which leads to errors and lost productivity.

## Supply chain resilience and traceability

Food and beverage supply chains are often complex and global. Geopolitical events and sudden shifts in demand can disrupt ingredients or packaging.

End-to-end traceability, from farm to fork, helps manage those risks. It helps detect food fraud, and allows a fast, targeted response when there's a quality issue. More in [Lot vs batch vs serial traceability](/post/lot-vs-serial-traceability).

## Where MES/MOM helps

| Challenge | How MES/MOM helps |
| --- | --- |
| **Food safety and quality** | Monitors critical parameters such as temperature and cooking time, and records every check against the batch |
| **Waste and give-away** | Tracks give-away by line and product in real time, and flags deviations early |
| **Yield and recipes** | Controls recipe execution and ingredient substitutions, and enforces shelf-life and FIFO rules |
| **Workforce** | Gives operators digital instructions at the right moment, and checks skills before work starts |
| **Traceability** | Records every ingredient lot and process step, so forward and backward traces take minutes |

Precise traceability also narrows recalls. When the system knows exactly which batches used an affected lot, fewer batches need to be recalled. Real-time inventory and production status feed better supply chain planning too.

Usability matters for adoption, particularly with younger operators: intuitive, mobile-friendly screens are more likely to be used consistently. More in [Connected worker vs MES](/post/connected-worker-one-app-not-five).

## My view

In food and beverage, I'd look first at yield and give-away, because that's where small improvements pay back fastest. A strong MES/MOM foundation helps food and beverage manufacturers move from reacting to problems to preventing them, which is what keeps consumers' trust over time.
