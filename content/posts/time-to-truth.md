The first question in any transformation is simple: where are you today?

The trouble is that most ways of answering it invite flattery. Ask a leadership team to rate its own maturity and the answer usually matches the last board presentation, not the shopfloor.

So I ask a different question.

**Line 3 stops at 02:14. A bearing has been running hot. When does the right person find out, and do they know why?**

The answer tells you more about a factory than any framework slide. I call it time to truth: how long it takes your factory to know what really happened, with enough context to act on it.

## Eventually: paper and Excel

The operator writes "machine fault, 40 minutes" on a downtime sheet. The sheet goes in a tray. At some point someone types it into a spreadsheet, if it gets typed up at all.

By the time anyone looks, the detail has gone. Which bearing? Was it the third time this month? Nobody can say, so the same failure happens again. And because every department keeps its own spreadsheet, every department has its own version of the number.

**How you'll recognise it:** travellers on clipboards, downtime on a whiteboard, and a morning meeting that starts with someone reading out yesterday's numbers from a notebook.

## End of hour or shift: ERP on the shopfloor

Production and scrap are typically booked into ERP at a terminal every hour, or at the end of the job or shift. It's a real step forward. The quantities are in the system of record, and finance can cost the order.

The truth arrives when someone books the confirmation. You know how many were good and what it cost. You don't know why 40 were scrapped or why the line stopped, because ERP's job is running the business. Watching what happens between an order starting and finishing is a different job.

**How you'll recognise it:** accurate order totals, and nobody able to explain them.

## After the fact: digitalised

Tablets replace the clipboards, and point solutions arrive: an OEE tool on a couple of lines, a checklist app, a maintenance app and a quality tool. Data still gets typed in by hand, and the tools don't talk to each other.

The truth arrives sooner, but always after the fact. Someone keys it in once the stop is over, from memory or a scribbled note, so it's rounded. The short stops never make it in, and the downtime record doesn't know which order was running.

**How you'll recognise it:** an app for every department, and a weekly report someone builds by copying between them.

## Right now: automatic data capture

The data stops being typed in. This is usually where MES/MOM arrives as the operational backbone, bringing production, quality, inventory and maintenance onto the same data and connecting the floor to ERP.

The stop is logged at 02:14 from the machine itself. The operator picks a reason at 02:16. The order and the material are already attached, because the system knows what was running.

**How you'll recognise it:** operators work from one screen, and nobody argues about the numbers any more.

## Before it happens: harmonious systems

A connected business. MES/MOM, ERP, PLM, scheduling, quality and data platforms work as one deliberate architecture. Each system does its own job and shares what it knows, and sensor coverage grows.

Now the bearing's temperature trend raises a warning at 23:40. Maintenance changes it during a planned stop on the night shift. Line 3 never goes down.

**How you'll recognise it:** maintenance gets scheduled because the data says so, not because the calendar does.

## Without asking: agentic and AI

AI agents work on trusted, contextualised data, inside clear guardrails. Systems make some of the decisions themselves, such as slowing a line to protect quality or raising the work order before anyone asks. People set the rules and handle the exceptions.

**How you'll recognise it:** honestly, you probably won't yet. Very few factories are genuinely here, whatever their marketing says.

## Truth arrives in order

You can't get to "before it happens" from "eventually". Prediction needs history, and history needs data captured accurately and automatically, with its context. That's what the "right now" stage builds.

That's true for AI that learns from operational data: predictive maintenance, predictive quality, optimisation and anomaly detection all depend on it. Generative AI is a little different. It arrives already trained, and it can work on the documents and know-how every factory already has, however messy. Procedures, manuals, maintenance notes and shift logs all count. So GenAI pilots can start in the earliest stages, running alongside the foundation work rather than waiting for it. I go further into this in [AI won't fix your factory](/post/the-future-of-smart-manufacturing-harnessing-the-power-of-mes-intelligence).

Most factories I see sit somewhere between "eventually" and "after the fact". A few are genuinely "right now" across the board. The jump to right now is usually the biggest single step in value, because it's where data becomes trustworthy and departments start working from the same picture.

## Your factory has a shape, not a single answer

This is the part a single maturity score hides. Time to truth is rarely the same everywhere.

It's common to find machine stops logged "right now" while quality checks still arrive "eventually" on paper. Or a plant with excellent dashboards and nobody who owns the process behind them. One site can be well ahead of another in the same group.

The slowest area sets the pace. Fast machine data doesn't help much if the reason, the quality result or the person who should act on it arrives a day later.

So ask the question area by area: process, data, systems, people, and analytics. The [readiness check](/tools/readiness-check) does exactly that, and gives you a time to truth for each one.

## How to use it

Ask it at the next leadership meeting. Pick a real event from last week, a stop or a quality escape, and trace when each person learnt about it, and what they knew.

Then ask it on the floor. You'll usually get two different answers, and the gap between them is where the work is.

So, if Line 3 stopped at 02:14 tonight, when would you know?
