You've been given the job of making the factory smarter. Maybe it came from the board. Maybe it came from your own frustration with paper and spreadsheets.

Either way, the first 90 days matter more than they look. Get them right and you'll have a funded first phase with a team behind it. Get them wrong and you'll spend a year piloting a tool that never scales.

This is how I'd use them.

## Before day one: agree what "smart" means here

A smart factory isn't science fiction, and it doesn't need an "Industry 4.0" label. My definition: a manufacturing environment where people, processes and technology work together, supported by data, to maximise efficiency, flexibility and quality. See [What is a smart factory?](/post/what-is-a-smart-factory)

Put that, or your own version, in front of leadership and get agreement on it. It stops the programme turning into "buy some AI" on week two.

## Days 1 to 30: understand where you really are

The first month is about listening and looking, not buying.

**Walk the shopfloor, on every shift.** Including nights. You'll see workarounds that never made it into the SOP, and paper in places nobody mentioned.

**Find your time to truth.** Pick a real stoppage from last week and trace when each person found out, and what they knew. Most factories land somewhere between "eventually" and "after the fact", and usually further back than they think. See [Time to truth](/post/time-to-truth).

**List every system and spreadsheet.** ERP, any MES or point solutions, quality tools, maintenance systems, the spreadsheets that actually run things, and who owns each one. It's often longer than anyone expected.

**Find the pain, with numbers.** Where do downtime and scrap cost the most? Where do people spend hours typing data from one place into another? Ask operators and supervisors as well as managers.

**By day 30 you should have:** a maturity assessment you'd defend in front of the shopfloor team, and a shortlist of the biggest losses with rough numbers attached.

## Days 31 to 60: decide where to start, and why

**Pick the first line.** Not necessarily the worst line, and not necessarily the easiest. Use the scorecard below.

**Map its process.** Current state first, with the people who do the work, then the target. Digitise a broken process and you get a faster broken process. See [Process before technology](/post/manufacturing-process-mapping).

**Check the master data for that line.** Are the routings detailed enough to say which machine each operation runs on, with realistic cycle times? If ERP has one operation called "Production", you've found your first work package. See [Your ERP master data isn't ready for MES](/post/erp-master-data-for-mes).

**Sketch the end state.** Where should this be in three years, across all four pillars of production, quality, inventory and maintenance, and across sites? You won't build it all now. But the first step should head towards it.

**By day 60 you should have:** a chosen first line, its current and target process, a master data gap list, and a one-page picture of the end state.

## Days 61 to 90: build the case and the team

**Write the business case.** Current costs, expected benefits, realistic costs including your own people's time, and how the risk is managed through phases and decision points. Leadership approves business impact, not features. See [How to build an MES business case](/post/mes-business-case).

**Secure a sponsor.** Someone senior enough to allocate resources and clear blockers. Without one, plant-level projects struggle.

**Name the team.** A project manager with real time, IT and operations together, super users from the line, and outside expertise where you don't have it. Backfill day jobs where you need to. See [Building the right team](/post/mes-implementation-team).

**Write requirements from your process maps**, not a wish list. Separate what you need now from what you might need later. Then you're ready to talk to vendors. See [How to choose an MES](/post/how-to-choose-an-mes).

**Find a quick win.** Something visible you can do now, such as replacing the quality check sheet on the first line, so people see progress while the bigger decision is made. Make sure it doesn't lock you into a tool that can't grow. See [Going paperless](/post/paperless-manufacturing).

**By day 90 you should have:** an approved first phase, a sponsor, a named team, requirements based on real processes, and one visible improvement on the shopfloor.

## Choosing the first line: a scorecard

Score each candidate line from 1 to 5 on each question. The highest total is usually the right place to start, but read the notes before you decide.

| Question | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| How much does its downtime or scrap cost? | Value you can measure is what funds the next phase |
| Is it the bottleneck, or close to it? | An hour gained on the bottleneck is an hour gained for the whole site |
| Is the process reasonably stable and documented? | You want to prove the system, not fight the process at the same time |
| Is the supervisor keen? | A champion on the line makes adoption much faster |
| Can the equipment be connected? | Automatic data capture is where the biggest early wins come from |
| Is it representative of other lines? | What you learn should carry over when you scale |
| Is the master data in decent shape, or quick to fix? | Poor master data is one of the most common causes of delay |

Two cautions. Don't pick a line purely because it's the worst performer if its problems are mostly mechanical. And don't pick the easiest line if nothing you learn there applies anywhere else.

## What not to do in the first 90 days

- **Don't buy software in month one.** You don't know your requirements yet.
- **Don't start with AI.** It needs the data foundation you haven't built yet. Run GenAI experiments on documents if you like, but don't make them the plan. See [AI won't fix your factory](/post/ai-needs-shopfloor-data).
- **Don't pilot a point solution you couldn't scale.** A pilot that becomes a silo costs you twice.
- **Don't leave the shopfloor out.** People support what they helped create. See [Resistance to change is guaranteed](/post/manufacturing-change-management).

## After day 90

From here the route is implementation: configure, connect the machines, go live on the first line, measure against the baseline, then build a template and scale. I go through it step by step in [How to modernise a factory](/post/how-to-modernise-a-factory).
